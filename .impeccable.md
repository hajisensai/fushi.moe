## Design Context

### Users
Fushi serves immersion learners who want to watch, read, listen, or play in a target language and turn encountered words into contextual Anki cards. The website's primary job is to explain the product quickly, establish trust, and help visitors download the correct build.

### Brand Personality
Open, trustworthy, and enthusiast-made. The site should feel like a polished community open-source project, never like an opaque commercial download funnel or a product waiting to reveal a paywall.

### Aesthetic Direction
Refined editorial minimalism with warm, content-led demonstrations. Keep the existing light/dark rhythm and restrained accent palette. Prefer correct platform names and clear text over decorative logo rows, generic SaaS cards, or app-store-style chrome.

### Design Principles
- State that Fushi is free and open source before asking visitors to download it.
- Make the source code visibly reachable on mobile as well as desktop.
- Use official product casing: Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS.
- Let real content demonstrations carry the personality; keep trust and navigation copy concise.
- Preserve accessible text labels even when an icon is present.
