Immersion only works if you're watching something you can actually get through. At the very start you understand nothing, and forcing it just makes you quit; watching with native-language subtitles all the way teaches nothing either — your eyes read your own language without you noticing. Fushi's answer is **two subtitle tracks with different jobs**:

- **Primary subtitle**: Japanese. Tappable, mineable — the track you *learn* from;
- **Secondary subtitle**: English (or Chinese), a **reference track** only, blurred by default, glanced at when needed.

## Attaching a secondary subtitle

The player's subtitle menu has a **Secondary subtitle** entry next to the primary one; pick a track in another language — an external file in the same folder or an embedded track in the mkv both work. (Fansub "bilingual" ASS files usually put both languages in one track; use those as the primary and leave the secondary empty.)

The secondary track has its own:

- **vertical position** (usually above the primary subtitle or at the top of the screen);
- **sync offset**: it follows the primary track's offset by default and can be set separately when the two come from different releases ([subtitles out of sync](/faq/subtitle-sync.en)).

## Four steps down

| Stage | Primary (Japanese) | Secondary (native) | What you're training |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Just starting | Shown | **Blurred**, reveal on hover / tap | Follow the plot from Japanese subtitles + lookups, glance at the translation when stuck |
| 2. Mostly following | Shown | **Hidden** | Japanese subtitles only; look up unknowns, mine the worthwhile ones |
| 3. Subtitles read smoothly | **Blurred**, reveal on pause / hover | Hidden | Listening — check the subtitle only when you didn't catch it |
| 4. Listening smoothly | Hidden | Hidden | Pure listening, occasionally verify in the subtitle list |

The settings live under **Video settings → Subtitles**: "Obscure" for the primary track and "Secondary subtitle obscure" for the secondary each offer off / blur / hide; "Reveal on pause or hover" decides whether a blurred subtitle shows temporarily when you pause, look up, hover (desktop) or tap (mobile). During playback the shortcuts **Cycle secondary subtitle obscure** and **Hide secondary subtitle** switch on the fly.

Don't follow the table rigidly: the first episodes of a show at stage 1, drifting into stage 2 as you go, then back a step for a harder show — all normal.

## A few principles

- **Glance at the secondary track, don't read it.** It's for checking the plot, not for reading; if you catch yourself reading whole sentences in your own language, hide it.
- **Turn it off as soon as you can.** The earlier the native-language crutch goes, the faster you progress; the point of the blur is that looking costs you a deliberate action.
- **Don't rush to drop the primary subtitle.** Watching with Japanese subtitles is the stage with the most input, and lookups and mining depend on it; only consider it at stage 3.
- **No translation available? Fine.** The secondary track is optional — with Japanese subtitles only, start at stage 2 and rely on lookups.

## Common situations

- **No track to choose in the secondary menu**: there's no second-language subtitle file in the folder and the mkv has a single track; find one on [Jimaku](https://jimaku.cc) or similar, or just go without.
- **The two tracks don't line up**: give the secondary track its own offset.
- **It's blurred but shows whenever I pause**: that's "Reveal on pause or hover" doing its job — turn it off to keep it blurred no matter what.
